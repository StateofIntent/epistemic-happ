### 1.2 `[V]` It is refuted by three lines of its own revision

| line | what it says |

**`[A]` The strongest form of the objection, and the fair counter to it.** One

pool as the thing that decides the outcome. A shared premise that **defines the
agreed object** is the one kind that cannot be discounted: if §8.3 is wrong,
both branches lose their target simultaneously, which is exactly the
independence the class claims to establish.

### 1.3 `[A]` What transfers, and it is two things

**(a) ROW 8.10's DESIGN CHOICE NOW HAS AN INSTANCE.** 8.10 folds the premise
edge in as a **critic-marked species** rather than as an author-declared
claim-to-claim link, and the recorded reason was that a new `LinkTypes` variant
forks the network. This supplies the reason that is about *evidence* rather
than about cost:

> **`[A]` An author's independence declaration is not durable.** Here it was
> declared, audited, withdrawn by the author, and **re-asserted one revision
> later in six places**, with the refutation sitting in the declaring table's
> own row. A system that stores *"these two claims are independently derived"*
> as an author-supplied property will serve a false one, in good faith, and
> nothing in the content will mark it. **Independence is a claim about two
> claims' ancestry, which is precisely the kind of claim this protocol makes a
> critic responsible for.**

**(b) `[A]` A SINGLE-SITED CORRECTION AGAINST A MULTI-SITED CLAIM LOSES, AND

> **`[A]` The rule, stated so it is usable here: before a correction is treated

`[A]` **And the failure mode is the 2026-11-24 one recurring across a revision

## 3. `[A]` One word was doing four jobs, and a per-claim role tag cannot catch it

The source's own detection event:

> `[Q]` *"What does gate mean in this context? A logic gate or a gate that
> opens or closes an entrance to somewhere?"*

One word had been carrying **four referents**: a **branch selector** choosing
between two available programs and committing per site; a **state holder**
deciding whether a program exists at all, thrown once; a **window**, a period
during which something is possible; and **`-gated`** as a modifier meaning
*controlled by*. The first two had been conflated, and the conflation had
**already produced a wrong question that was answered on the record** — the
answer being *the selector, not the switch*, a distinction needed only because
one word covered both.

**`[A]` THIS IS NOT THE ROLE MISMATCH 8.2 CARRIES, AND THE DIFFERENCE IS WHERE
THE TAG SITS.** 8.2 tags **a claim** with its mechanism's role and catches *this
claim answers a different question than it appears to.* This defect is
**per-term across claims**: every statement is correct about its own referent,
and the error exists only in the relation between them. Role tags applied to
each claim separately report no conflict at all. So the object needed is a
**report, not a field** — group claims by a shared term, flag terms whose
claims carry different role tags. Row **8.17**.

### 3.1 `[V]` Measured here first, because this repository has the same word doing the same thing

Counting senses of *gate* across `docs/*.md`, `dna/*/src/*.rs` and
`scripts/*.mjs` on 2026-11-25, **four distinct referents are in live use**:

| sense | refers to | instance |
|---|---|---|
| **a detector that blocks** | a CI or document check that reddens a merge | *"A gate that does not run is indistinguishable from one that was never written, except that it reads like coverage"*; *"A gate with 63 false reds is broken rather than weak"* |
| **a precondition modifier** | *conditional on* | `tenure-gated`, `credential-gated`, *"gate on step 4 having produced real reports"* |
| **an admission door before an outward act** | a human prerequisite about timing and audience | *"both rows gate a funding conversation"*; *"the gate's purpose is to precede people installing, not to forbid describing the work"* |
| **a read-layer refusal** | withholding a reading from a reader | row 6.28: *"THIS GATE HAS TWO INPUTS, AND THE CODE ALREADY LEANS ON THE CHEAPER ONE"* |

**`[A]` THE FINDING IS WHICH SENSE IS ABSENT.** **None of the four is a branch
selector** — the sense the source's confusion was about. This protocol *has*
branch selectors (`CritiqueMode`, `AntibodyPatternKind`, `CritiqueSpecies`,
tabled in the third companion's §5) and **calls none of them a gate.** So the
exposure runs the other way here: the selectors are named precisely and the
refusals share one word.

> **`[A]` One argument in our record already turned on telling two of them
> apart** — the outward-facing gate, contested in exactly these terms and
> resolved by naming the referent: *"THE DISTINCTION IS THE DOOR, NOT THE
> RECIPIENT."* That is this item arriving before it had a name, and it is the
> only evidence here that the report would find something.

**`[A]` The detector does not transfer.** The equivocation was caught by **a
reader asking what a word meant** — not by a sourcing check, not by an
internal-consistency check, not by the author. §8.3 already counted this shape:
of 26 corrections in the source's appendix, **7 name the interlocutor** and 2 a
self-audit. The largest named detector is another person.

**`[U]` Both load-bearing caveats, kept:** the review states its causal sequence

### 4.1 `[A]` What transfers, and it makes 8.13 decidable in halves

Row **8.13** is this import's open complaint that **a confidence label without a
regime of validity is nearly worthless.** `[A]` The sequence result adds the
distinction 8.13 does not make. A regime can be **parametric** — *this holds for
inputs in this range* — or **ordinal** — *this holds at this phase, after that
one and before the next.* The source's own corrected rows include both kinds:
*"true in the invertebrate literature circa 2007"* is ordinal; *"far field
only"* is parametric.

**`[V]` MEASURED HERE, AND THE PROTOCOL HAS EXACTLY ONE ORDINAL RULE.**
`action.timestamp()` appears **10 times** in `dna/integrity/src/lib.rs`
(`10a5107`). Of those:

- **five are rate windows** — `CRITIQUE_`, `ANTIBODY_PATTERN_`,
  `SYNAPTIC_LINK_`, `REINFORCEMENT_` and
  `ATTESTATION_GRANT_WINDOW_SECS_VALIDATION`, each computing a `window_start`
  and counting the author's own recent actions back along their source chain
  (`:1763`, `:1912`, `:2437`, `:2538`, `:2615`). **A rate limit is not a phase
  rule**: it bounds how fast, not what may follow what.
- **`validate_pre_registration`'s ordering checks are the only phase rule**
  (`:1377`–`:1443`) — the registration is bound to its committing action's
  timestamp, and the reveal deadline is compared against **that action's time**
  rather than against any declared field.

> **`[A]` SO AN ORDINAL REGIME ALREADY HAS A CARRIER, AND IT IS THE ONE 8.4's
> AUDIT SAYS MAY HAVE A GOVERNOR AT ALL.** `action.timestamp()` is in the record
> every peer holds, so binding a label's validity to **order** breaks no peer
> agreement. Binding it to a **range** needs a clock, and `SettlementPolicy`
> **refused a clock explicitly**. **Ordinal is the cheap half of 8.13 and
> parametric is the expensive half** — which means 8.13 can be decided in two
> parts instead of deferred whole. Row **8.17**'s second half.

**`[A]` And the critique shape it suggests, which costs nothing to state:**
*your supporting observations conflict only under a static reading — are they
snapshots of one sequence?* A conflicting-evidence set normally reads as
evidence against a claim; under this reading it can be evidence that **the
variable was mis-typed**. `CritiqueMode`'s five variants address content, not
the type of a variable, so this is parked as a candidate `CritiqueSpecies` once
8.12 is seeded — **not** a sixth mode, which would fork the network (8.1).
