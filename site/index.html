Peer-to-peer epistemic infrastructure

Disagreement with a name on it.

The cost of producing a plausible claim collapsed. The cost of checking one did not. That inverts what is scarce — not information, but attributable disagreement. This is a protocol that stores typed disagreement instead of scores, cannot delete anything, and publishes its own failure rates.

What changed

Asserting became free. Checking did not.

A language model will produce a confident, well-sourced-looking paragraph on any subject for a fraction of a cent. Verifying that paragraph still costs a human being their attention, their expertise, and often a week.

Every mechanism we have for sorting claims was designed when the opposite was true — when producing a claim was expensive and the problem was finding it.

Four mechanisms, and what they do now
MechanismWhat it was forWhat it does now
RankingSurfacing the scarce good thingSorts by volume — the thing that just became free
ModerationRemoving the rare bad actorAdjudicates at a volume no human can read, so it is automated, and automation deletes heterodox-but-true
ReputationIdentifying who is reliableJudges the speaker — exactly what cheap assertion makes cheap to fake
EngagementMeasuring interestA one-dimensional projection of a many-dimensional disagreement

All four share one assumption: that the expensive thing is the claim. It is not any more. The expensive thing is a specific, attributable, well-aimed objection — and we have no system that stores one as a first-class object. Community Notes is the closest thing in production, and it still collapses to a vote on whether a note is helpful.

The scarce good is no longer information. It is disagreement with a name on it.

The one move

Make typed disagreement the primitive.

Not a reaction to a post — an entry with its own author, target, evidence and kind. Four things follow, and each is enforced rather than encouraged.

01

Critiques are typed, never flattened

Five kinds ship. A system that collapses them into one number has thrown away the only information that distinguishes a bad study from an unwelcome result. The taxonomy is extensible at runtime, so the network can speciate new kinds of objection without a fork.

02

There is no score, and that is structural

No karma, no stars, no trust index, no sorted leaderboard. Aggregation is allowed only when a named agent publishes it as their own reading — which makes it a promise you can argue with instead of arithmetic you cannot. There is nothing to game because there is no number to move.

03

Nothing is deleted

No delete operation is accepted at all — the capability is absent from the network's rules, not merely unexposed. A critique cannot be buried and a prediction cannot be quietly withdrawn. This is also the protocol's sharpest cost, and it is stated plainly rather than hidden.

04

Commitments are sealed, with no registrar

A prediction can be committed with its question and deadline public from the moment of commitment and its content sealed. There is no judge and no registrar — only a seal, a clock, and a denominator that refuses to shrink when somebody declines to reveal. You cannot pre-select a favourable adjudicator, because there is none.

The primitive, in detail

Five kinds of objection.

A critique names which kind it is. The kinds are peers — no order is implied here and none exists in the protocol, because an ordering would be the ranking the design exists to refuse.

Experiential

I tried this and…

Methodological

The design is flawed because…

Logical

This contradicts itself.

Evidential

Your source doesn't say that.

Phenomenological

My experience differs.

Critiques target statements, not people. The protocol refuses to let an objection name an agent as its subject — because that would be the reputation mark the no-score rule exists to prevent.

Where it sits

Four neighbours, and what each one left on the table.

None of these is a competitor. Three of them solved a part of this problem properly, and the part they did not solve is the same part each time.

Four comparisons, one at a time — click or tap a title to open it.

Same geometry, different scarcity

N4L is a notation for notes that keeps what prose keeps: direction, type, context, nuance. SSTorytime is its reference implementation. The goal is explicitly anti-flattening, and this protocol takes the geometry wholesale — four link semantics, context as a first-class dimension, no collapse to a score. What N4L is not built for is adversarial multi-agent use, and it does not claim to be. It is one author's notes, trusted because they are yours.

It is additive, not competitive. The export exists so that the thing Burgess built to read knowledge graphs can read this one.

N4L solved the notation. This is an attempt at the part that only appears when the notes have to survive other people.

The box nobody ticked

The Semantic Web's original programme had three parts: machine-readable meaning, provenance, and trust. The first two got standards. The third got a name — the Oh yeah? button, where a browser would ask why should I believe this — and never got built. Solid delivered the half the web most needed, data sovereignty, and does not claim to attempt trust semantics. The full argument, with the 1997 specification and the layer stack →

The original programmeShipped elsewhereHere
Typed relations between thingsRDF / OWLYes — typed links, 31 kinds
Provenance: who said this, whenPartlyEvery entry carries its author and action; nothing is anonymous
Data sovereigntySolid podsYes — each agent is a node
Trust: why should I believe thisNobodyThe whole object: typed disagreement, attached to the claim, permanently
Disagreement as dataNo primitive anywhereFive types, extensible
A reading you can argue withNoAggregation requires a named aggregator, so every reading is a promise

And the axis where it is genuinely opposed to Solid, stated first because it is the first thing a serious reader raises. Solid exists partly so you can remove your data. This protocol makes erasure structurally impossible — no delete operation is accepted by the network at all, which means a right-to-erasure request cannot be complied with on this network by anyone. It is a deliberate trade: a record that can be edited by the party with the most to lose is not a record. A deployment for the general public would have to confront this; the pilot confronts it by being small, consenting and informed.

The complementary half

Mattereum binds physical assets to claims using Ricardian contracts — a document simultaneously readable by a human, a court, and a machine. Its hard problem is the warranty: somebody must assert a fact about an object, and bear consequences if it is false. This protocol is built on the same shape and solves a different half. Its correlative witness is explicitly not a Ricardian contract — no legal force, no counterparty obligation — but it is the durable, attributable, critiquable record a warranty regime needs and a review system cannot provide.

Mattereum needsTodayWhat this adds
A warranty asserted by a named partyRicardian contractCompatible — a claim here is already authored and permanent
Somewhere disputes live that isn't a review scoreMissingTyped critique, attached to the asset's claim, undeletable
Provenance that survives the platformOn-chainA witness that survives the counterparty deleting their side
Seeing that a warranty was challenged, and by whomMissingThe critique graph, with the challenger named
No platform adjudicating who is rightThe aimAn invariant: there is no judge and no score

An asset passport gets a membrane, and disputes about that asset become typed critiques with provenance instead of star ratings.

Neither does the other's job, and the two compose without either changing its invariants.

A sensemaking tool, not another feed

The flood is usually described as a volume problem. It is a cost-asymmetry problem, and that distinction decides what a tool has to do. A tool that helps must make the expensive thing accumulate and the cheap thing worthless. This one does both by construction.

  • Volume buys nothing. There is no ranking to climb, no feed position, no score. Ten thousand generated claims produce ten thousand unranked entries and move nothing.
  • The critique is the asset. A typed objection with evidence attached is the thing that persists and the thing a reader navigates by.
  • Friction is per-identity and deliberate. Rate limits on writing are budgeted, and the specification states plainly that they bound throughput per identity and not the cost of making new identities. That is the honest limit, and it is written in the spec rather than in an FAQ.
  • A reader's lens is their own. Any filter is supplied by the caller, never a protocol default, so there is no single algorithmic view to capture.

For a human navigating a domain where most text is now synthetic, the useful question is not what is popular but what has survived someone's informed objection, and who objected.

That question has no answer on any platform today. Here it is the primary read.

Against a curated record

Four properties, none of which requires anyone to be honest.

Failure modeThe usual defenceThe mechanism here
Quietly retracting a prediction that failedScreenshots, memoryA sealed commitment with a public question and deadline, and a denominator that refuses to shrink when the author declines to reveal. The non-answer is itself countable
Burying the correctionWe updated the articleNo delete operation exists. The retraction is a new entry beside the claim; both stand
The objection being drownedModerationNo ranking, so volume cannot drown anything — there is no ordering to flood
Attacking the critic instead of the critiqueFact-check labelsCritiques target statements, not people, and the protocol refuses to let an objection name an agent as its subject
The algorithm decidedTransparency reportsAny aggregate requires a named aggregator who owns it as a promise; a protocol-computed verdict is forbidden

Most dishonesty in public claim-making is not a lie; it is a selective record. If someone's failed predictions never enter the sample, their hit rate is unfalsifiable. Here the unrevealed commitment stays in the count — so the sample cannot be curated by the person being measured.

Why believe any of it

The method is the differentiator.

Most epistemic-infrastructure projects ask for trust in exactly the currency they claim to be replacing. This one has a method you can check.

Nulls get published

When a network test fails intermittently, the rate is published as a lower bound with its denominator stated and the ambiguous cases named — rather than retried until green. The repository records a slow-propagation distribution with sixteen-plus measured values, including the ones that weaken the case.

Checks are proven capable of failing

A gate that cannot go red is treated as absent. New checks are deliberately broken and watched failing, and the number of ways is recorded. An exit code is not a measurement is a standing rule with its own instances.

Figures are counted, not estimated

Every number on this page was re-measured against the source tree on the day it was written. Where a figure could not be reproduced from the code, the smaller verifiable number is the one printed.

Corrections are recorded in place

Including the embarrassing ones. The open-work file carries its own retracted claims with the reason, and a correction accepted and then quietly re-asserted is treated as a finding in its own right.

Rule changes are visible forks

The integrity zome's hash is the network's identity, so changing a rule cannot be a silent migration. Change one invariant and you have visibly forked rather than quietly migrated.

The whole history is public

580 merged pull requests, each with its reasoning, its measurements and its reversals. Nothing described here is a roadmap item you have to take on faith.

An epistemic tool that cannot show its own error rate is asking for the trust it claims to be replacing. This one can show it, and does, unprompted.

An independent check

The biology that arrived at the same answers.

This protocol was compared, line by line, against an adversarial review of how biological tissue decides its own size and form — an inquiry with no interest in epistemics whatsoever. Four of its structural results are things this protocol had already decided for Promise Theory reasons, and two of its distinctions found real defects in the code.

Honesty first. The biology below is drawn from an external document, is not verified here, and nothing in the protocol depends on any of it being true. What transfers is structure, not fact — and that is exactly why the convergence counts: two systems solving how do you determine a quantity without storing it arrived at the same answers from unrelated premises.

Where the biology derived a decision already taken

Biological resultThis protocol's decisionWhy it matters
Thymus vs spleen No canonical comparative score; aggregation requires a named aggregator Multiple transplanted thymus grafts each grow to full size — excess is not suppressed. Spleen grafts each stay small, total mass matching one spleen. A spleen must normalise over its whole population, and Promise Theory's result is that an agent cannot normalise over a population it cannot see — so this protocol cannot be spleen-shaped. A design choice turned out to be a derivation.
The growth plate determines an adult size with no stored target and no comparison get_standing stores nothing; standing is the product of a read Final length = divisions remaining × length gained per division, and neither factor knows the product. Both systems achieve determinacy without specification — which is how the hardest invariant, atrophy without deletion, was resolved: the reader is the governor.
Exhaustion is not comparison — a budget stops a process by being empty, not by being compared against zero Conductance decays toward zero at read time Nothing is deleted, nothing is blocked, nothing refuses — an un-reinforced link simply stops being load-bearing. A limit with no reader. The biology explains why that shape is coherent rather than a dodge: a quantity that runs out needs no judge.
Removing the immune system caused scarring — it did not prevent it Judgement is relocated, not removed Depleting macrophages in a regenerating limb produced fibrosis in 10 of 10 animals, with the construction cells present the whole time. The common objection to this design is you have just deleted moderation. The sharpest answer: removing the deciding layer does not give you neutrality, it gives you the default failure mode. What is removed here is the anonymous judge, not judgement.

The distinctions that found real defects

These are not analogies. Each is a borrowed question that produced a measured finding in a 14,000-line codebase.

Borrowed distinctionWhat it found here
Recorder versus governorAn audit of every declared-time field in the protocol: 18 self-declared timestamps with exactly one bound to anything the network can check. Eighteen recorders, one governor.
Mark versus readoutThe rule name the reader, or admit there is none — which found two entry fields with no reader at any layer, including one whose type promises resolvability and which every test harness sets to null. Both now say so in the code.
Compensation has a trigger thresholdNamed the attack shape on the protocol's own write limits: the way past a cap of 20 per window is twenty-one agents, not twenty-one critiques. It is also why the one decaying limit cannot be escaped the same way — a slope has no threshold to hide under.
A quantity can have a correct value rather than a better directionA sort of the protocol's named constants into dial, placeholder, specification — and a fourth class nobody had: a threshold whose resolution is coarser than the distinction it is meant to make. One shipped warning turned out to be in it.

The thing that catches errors is a specific objection from someone who read it, and no amount of automated checking substitutes.

The external review's own appendix counted who caught its errors: of 26 recorded corrections, seven name an interlocutor and two a self-audit. The largest single detector of its own mistakes was another person.

Honest limits

Two operators is not a network.

One day of adversarial use is an anecdote, not a measurement, and it should not be read as more. The protocol is not at scale and is not close. Three specific weaknesses, stated plainly:

  1. macOS cannot auto-update. Measured on the shipped artefact: the bundle has no code signature, so the updater can never accept a successor build. Mac participants hand-install. This is a blocker on adoption, not a nice-to-have.
  2. Rate limits do not bound identity creation, and the specification says so. Sybil resistance and spam defence are treated as distinct properties, and only the second is addressed.
  3. Erasure is impossible, which is a legal and ethical cost before it is a technical one. A right-to-erasure request cannot be complied with on this network by anyone.

For funders and collaborators

What the money is for.

Layers one through eight exist. The ninth — a cohort of independent operators — is the only one that cannot be built by writing more code, and it is the one that turns every claim on this page from an argument into a measurement.

In order

  1. Always-on infrastructure, so the network exists when nobody is watching.
  2. Remove the macOS install blocker.
  3. Recruit and pay ten to fifteen operators on their own machines for a fixed period.
  4. Publish the benchmark, with nulls included.

The bet is narrow and testable: that the scarce good is attributable disagreement, and that a system which stores it rather than scoring it will be more useful than one more ranking surface. That bet is wrong if typed critique turns out to be something people will not write.

The cohort is how you find out, and it is what the money is for.

What adoption would change

01

A domain gets a memory that survives its platform

Any field where practitioners know things that never reach the literature — rehabilitation protocols, trades knowledge, rare-disease experience — currently loses that knowledge when a forum dies or an algorithm changes. A membrane does not.

02

Who objected, and on what grounds becomes navigable

Today you can ask what is popular, what is recent, and what a moderator permitted. You cannot ask what survived informed objection. For a reader facing synthetic text at volume, that is the only question that still discriminates.

03

Public claim-making acquires a denominator

Confident prediction is cheap in public life because the failures leave the sample. A public question, a sealed answer and a count that cannot be curated is a small, mechanical change with a large incentive consequence.
